## How it measures tilt

The accelerometer feels every acceleration, including gravity. When the board lies flat, gravity pulls straight down on the Z axis, so Z reads about 9.81 m/s² and X and Y read near zero. Tip the board and some of that pull moves onto X or Y. The firmware turns those three numbers into roll and pitch angles with basic trigonometry.

The gyroscope measures rotation speed in degrees per second around each axis. It does not know which way is down; it only knows how fast you are turning right now.

## Why it uses both

The accelerometer gives the right angle on average, but it jumps whenever you shake or move the board. The gyro is smooth and fast, but adding up its readings over time drifts. The firmware uses a **complementary filter**: each step it trusts the gyro for 98% of the change and nudges toward the accelerometer's angle for the remaining 2%. You get smooth motion that does not drift away in roll and pitch.

## The axes on the board

The GY-521 has small X and Y arrows printed on it. Roll is rotation around X (tipping a wing down), pitch is rotation around Y (nose up or down), and yaw is rotation around Z (turning left or right on the table).

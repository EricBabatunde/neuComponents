## How time of flight works

The sensor fires short pulses of infrared light and counts how long the reflection takes to come back. Light travels about 300 mm per nanosecond, so a target 1 m away returns light after roughly 6.7 ns. The VL53L1X measures that with an array of very fast light detectors called SPADs.

## Reading the sweep profile

The VL53L1X returns one distance per reading, not an image. To see a shape, sweep the sensor slowly across it by hand. Each reading becomes a dot on the profile, newest on the right. Pointing at a box on a table, then past its edge to the floor, shows two flat levels with a step between them. The height of the step is the height of the box.

## Why edges look like ramps

Because the sensor sees a cone, the reading near an edge mixes the near and far surfaces. The closer the sensor is to the edge, the narrower the cone and the sharper the step. Move closer to get a crisper profile.

## Going further

The VL53L1X can shrink its detector to a region of interest as small as 4×4 of its 16×16 SPADs and move that region around. Stepping the region across the array gives a coarse grid of distances without moving the sensor. That mode is a good next experiment once the basic profile makes sense.
